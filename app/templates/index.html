Interpretation

automatic, from the solved values

    This tool solves a water pipe network. You describe the network (reservoirs, junctions and pipes), press Solve, and it returns the hydraulic head and pressure at every junction, the flow, velocity and head loss in every pipe, and two diagrams. It uses the Nodal Head Correction Method (Newton-Raphson), the same solver validated against EPANET to within 0.001%.

    Quick start

    1. The Solver tab already has the Single-loop network loaded and solved, so its results show what an answer looks like.
    2. Pick another network from Preset, or edit the Nodes and Pipes tables directly.
    3. Click Solve network. Every output updates.
    4. Use + node / + pipe to grow the network, or the row delete button to remove an item.

    What each input means

    • reservoir: a source with a fixed water level. Set its fixed head (m). Every network needs at least one. Elevation and demand are ignored.
    • junction: a connection or delivery point. Set its elevation (m) and demand (m³/s, positive for water drawn off). Leave fixed head blank; the tool computes the head.
    • pipe: connects two nodes. Set length (m), diameter (m) and roughness. Flow direction is solved, not entered.

    Solver options

    • Head-loss model: Hazen-Williams (roughness = coefficient C, typically 100-150) or Darcy-Weisbach (roughness = absolute ε in metres).
    • Tolerance: how small the flow imbalance must get (default 1e-6 m³/s).
    • Max iterations: a safety cap (default 100).

    Reading the results

    • The KPI strip shows the headline numbers: convergence status, iterations, residual, solve time, the lowest pressure and the fastest pipe.
    • The diagram colours junctions by pressure head and arrows show solved flow direction.
    • Head is total energy level; pressure head is head minus elevation, the pressure available for service.
    • The Interpretation panel explains the result and flags low pressures or fast pipes.

    The Nodal Head Correction Method treats the hydraulic head at each junction as the unknown and adjusts all heads together until water is conserved at every node.

    How it works

    1. Start every unknown junction head at the average reservoir level.
    2. From the head difference across each pipe, compute its flow using the chosen head-loss law.
    3. At each junction, sum the inflows and subtract the demand; the leftover is the continuity residual.
    4. If the largest residual is below tolerance, stop. Otherwise build the Jacobian, solve one sparse linear system for the head corrections, apply them, and repeat.

    Being a Newton-Raphson method, the error roughly squares each step near the solution, so it converges in a handful of iterations regardless of network size. Pipe flow direction falls out of the solution rather than being assumed.

    How it is validated

    Every network here was also solved in EPANET (the U.S. EPA reference engine) through the WNTR Python package. Across networks from 4 to over 120 nodes the maximum difference in head is about 0.001%, far below the 0.1% target. The small network was additionally confirmed by an independent Hardy Cross hand calculation.

    Scope

    Steady-state analysis: constant demands, no transients, no minor losses, no pumps or valves. Water is incompressible and flow fully turbulent through rigid, leak-free pipes, with demand only at nodes.

    References: Rossman (2000); Todini & Pilati (1988); Swamee & Jain (1976); Klise et al. (2017).
